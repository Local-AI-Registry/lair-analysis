Option 1: Keep Studio 3 on the website

Studio 3 keeps managing your site and runs their platform AEO. We stop all website work and focus fully on everything off your website.

Pros
  • No change to your web team or how your site is run
  • No overlap or conflicting work on your site
  • Our off-page work keeps going: your Google profile, reviews, photos, posts and PR
Cons
  • Your SEO and AEO progress will be capped, because off-page work can only go so far without the website keeping up
  • Platform updates are the same for every customer, not built around your practice
  • The issues below get fixed on Studio 3's schedule, not ours

Option 2: We handle all of it

We take over web management along with SEO and AEO, so one team owns your site and your search presence, on the website and off it.

Pros
  • One team accountable for results
  • Faster: fixes and new pages go live every week, built for your practice
  • Every page kept current, not just two procedures
  • Website and off-page work move together
  • We fix every issue listed below
Cons
  • A short transition and handoff of your website from Studio 3 to us

Fix 2

Your articles are credited to writers, not you

All 42 articles on your blog name one of five writers as the author in the page code. Patients don't see the name, but Google and AI assistants read it, and it also shows as "Written by" when an article is shared. The writers' accounts are tied to studio3enterprise.com email addresses.

The same articles say "Dr. Jeffrey Lisiecki explains" in their description, so the code contradicts itself. For medical topics, Google and AI give more weight to content written or reviewed by a qualified doctor.

What we'd do: credit every article to you as the author or medical reviewer, with your credentials and a link to your bio page.
Fix 3

Your pages compete with each other

When two of your pages go after the same search, Google has to pick one, and the credit gets split. Google ranking data shows this happening on your site today.

Buccal fat removal: two full pages

"Buccal Fat Removal Manhattan" and "Buccal Fat Pad Removal NYC" are separate pages on the same procedure. Both show up in Google for buccal fat searches, so neither gets the full credit.

Ethnic rhinoplasty: a blog post beats the procedure page

In Google ranking data, searches like "ethnic rhinoplasty NYC" bring up your blog article on page one, not your Ethnic Rhinoplasty page. Patients land on the article instead of the page built to book them.

6 blank copies still live

Old, empty copies of six procedure pages sit at second web addresses. Your real pages are fine, but the blank copies still compete with them. Four are surgeries filed under "Non-Surgical."

Fix 4

Your topical map has big gaps

Google and AI judge you on how fully you cover each procedure. Your site covers rhinoplasty well, but the rest is uneven.

  • Your main Facelift page is empty. It's only a list of links, with no write-up. Six other section pages work the same way: Face, Neck & Jawline, Breast, Body, Men and Non-Surgical.
  • Otoplasty has photos but no page. You have an otoplasty before-and-after gallery, but no page explaining the procedure.
  • Many of your procedures don't show up in Google. Your site appears for about 200 searches in Google ranking data, but not for "facelift NYC" or any deep plane facelift search, and nothing for preservation rhinoplasty, eyelid surgery, Morpheus8 or BBL.
What we'd do: build a full map of your procedures, write a real overview for every section page starting with Facelift, add the missing procedure pages, and link each topic together so Google sees the full picture.
Fix 5

Your site isn't connected to your profiles across the web

AI assistants decide who you are by connecting your website to your profiles elsewhere. You have strong ones: a verified RealSelf profile, The Aesthetic Society's surgeon finder, Doximity (which lists your published research in Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery), Zocdoc and Vitals.

Your site code links to only two: Instagram and YouTube. And the entry that describes you as the doctor has no name filled in, so your training, memberships and awards aren't clearly tied to you.

What we'd do: fix your doctor profile in the code and connect it to every verified profile you have, then add procedure and FAQ code to each procedure page, so Google and AI see one clear, trusted doctor.
